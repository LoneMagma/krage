# Comparable games and krage's positioning

Reviewed 7 September 2026. This is a documentation/community comparison, not a hands-on performance benchmark of competitors. Evidence of preference below is anecdotal; it does not prove market demand or uniqueness.

## Useful lessons

**Krunker:** its published guide puts movement practice, aim settings, custom bindings and training beside combat. The useful lesson is to make improvement understandable and repeatable. Krage already offers target bots, difficulty selection, a benchmark and a separate controls reference. This pass adds predictable slide steering and a real equip window. Full remapping and a short optional movement/aim course remain worthwhile; copying movement exploits or endlessly increasing speed would conflict with the requested feel. The guide is not reliable evidence for precise current balance values, so none were copied. [Published controls/practice guide](https://krunker.io/guides/controls/).

**War Brokers:** its developer page presents extensive weapon/vehicle variety, customization, missions and performance options. The useful lesson for krage is clear weapon roles and access to appearance/progression from the main menu. The implemented locker, cosmetic finish previews and automatic authored challenges adopt that organizational idea within a much smaller arsenal. The same page contains older player comments about oversized maps, weak-PC load/FPS problems, account friction and sparse nearby servers. Those support testing compact arenas, easy local practice and regional connectivity; they are not a representative survey or evidence of current defects. [Developer page and community comments](https://warbrokers.itch.io/war-brokers).

Do not import vehicles, a large weapon catalog, crates or full environmental destruction into this build. Each would increase balance, download, rendering or server complexity before krage's current loop is proven enjoyable.

## Three product promises to validate

1. **Grounded movement with room for mastery.** Firm stops, aim aligned with hit detection, and a short steerable slide make route choice and timing matter. This is intended to serve players who enjoy expressive FPS movement but want readable fights. Validate with novice and experienced players: can both intentionally slide through a route, stop on cover and explain why a shot missed? Compare movement feel ratings and accidental-slide frequency. Do not call it better until those sessions support it.
2. **A complete frag loop from three weapons and one blade.** Choose a primary each spawn, trade SMG mobility/AK range/TWIN close-range commitment, and use EDGE when closing distance is worthwhile. Compact vertical routes create repeatable duels and skill shots. Validate encounter time, spawn deaths, weapon pick rates, per-weapon success and rematch choice. Avoid balancing only from bot matches.
3. **Easy access with fair personal identity.** Local practice works without waiting for a populated server; settings expose a low-cost render mode; appearance unlocks do not change combat statistics. Validate cold-start time, p95 frame time on named low-end devices, challenge completion without unnatural farming, and return sessions. Online access still depends on the unfinished client/server integration.

These are a combined direction, not three industry-first inventions. Cosmetics alone are ordinary retention infrastructure, not a defensible selling point. The strongest candidate distinction is the combination of bounded movement, a deliberately small spawn-based arsenal and an accessible short-match loop. Community feedback and real device/network tests must decide whether that combination is wanted.

## Refinements adopted in this build

- Keep the world readable with distinct map landmarks and stable ally/enemy/action colors.
- Give weapon transitions a consistent timing window and distinctive sound, and match SVG finish previews to 3D equipment.
- Keep bot practice useful while blocking benchmark/target farming from challenge rewards.
- Build room authority around shared combat rules instead of accepting client hits or scores.
- Preserve identical operator rigs/collision and a cosmetics-only progression contract.

The next learning step is a small consented playtest cohort spanning experienced arena players, occasional FPS players and actual low-end machines. No survey has been run and no retention or purchase demand is claimed.
